By the hour the lamps woke, Mira Voss had already climbed nine ladders.

Veyra went dark in layers: first the harbor, then the market stairs, then the crooked lanes of the Old Quarter, where the lanterns were older than the streets that held them. Mira was not allowed to touch anything brighter than a street lamp. Master Hale said that was a rule. Mira thought it was a habit.

She lit the last lamp on Tallow Row and was reaching for the ladder when the lantern behind her said, very softly, "Mira."

She did not turn at once. Lanterns did not speak. Everyone knew that, the way everyone knew the tide came in twice a day.

"Mira Voss," it said again, thin as a wick about to go out. "Don't let them."

"Let them what?" she whispered.

The flame guttered. In the glass, for one heartbeat, she saw a woman's hand pressing something small into the light, and then the picture burned away and the lantern was only a lantern.

Footsteps rang on the stones behind her, too quick for a man of Master Hale's years, and yet it was Master Hale who stepped out of the dark with his coat half buttoned and his face the color of ash.

"Step away from that lamp," he said.
